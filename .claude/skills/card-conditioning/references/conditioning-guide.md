# Pokémon TCG Conditioning Guide

For eBay listing prep. Honest grades, return-safe without being fussy. Locked 31 August 2026.
Scale we list with: NM · LP · MP · HP · DMG. No plus or minus.
We follow Big Orbit Cards’ Pokémon condition guide for NM / LP / MP / HP, with the house lines below so two people (or a bot and Connor) land on the same step. DMG is our floor below HP.

## How we look

Front and back. Card-size first, then a close look. Do not grade from a tight crop alone.
Order: corners, edges, surface. Then note centering.

## Not a grade hit

Off-centering, unless it is so extreme a buyer would think the card is miscut. Mention it in the listing if it is obvious. Do not drop NM for it.
Scanner streaks (vertical RGB / yellow lines), scan rainbow or moiré on holos, factory print lines.
Dust, lint, or a fibre that will wipe.

## Count as wear

Whitening, nicks, chips, silvering, scratches that survive a dry wipe, creases, stains, peeling, writing, liquid.
A countable wear point is a distinct nick, chip, whitening patch, or scratch, not every pixel on a zoomed corner.

### NM. Near Mint or Better

Not pack-fresh mint. A few minor imperfections, only visible on close exam, and limited in number.
House line: fewer than three countable wear points, and closer to NM than LP.
Allowed: limited light edge or corner wear; a few extremely small nicks; a few light scratches or scuffs; sparse holo-edge twinkling; slight holo warp.
Not NM: three or more distinct wear points, even if each is small; anything obvious at a glance; any crease, tear, peel, writing, or liquid.
Calibration: Pokégear 3.0, Sword & Shield 174/202 (Doc01560003 / 004). A couple of micro back nicks on close look. NM.
Retired: “anything not fully clean on close look lists as LP.” That was over-harsh and does not match Big Orbit NM.
Holos / reverse holos: scratches must not show silver through the foil. Edge twinkling must be sparse.

### LP. Light Play

Minor wear. Some of it may be noticeable at a glance. None of it severe, and not present in large numbers. No crease, tear, peel, writing, or liquid.
House line: three or more light flaws, or one or two flaws that are distinct at a glance (a real whitening patch, not a pin speck), without going all the way around the card.

### Calibration

Pikachu, Sword & Shield 065/202 (Doc01560001 / 002). Four corner-tip nicks. LP.
Counter Catcher, Crimson Invasion 91/111 (Doc01560007 / 008). Multiple pin nicks. LP.
Devoured Field, Burning Shadows 93/111 reverse (Doc01560005 / 006). Glance-visible BR / bottom whitening. LP, not MP.
Holos: still no silver through scratches. Twinkling can be more extensive than NM, but should not be loud while the card is at rest.

### MP. Moderate Play

Wear that knocks it out of LP: immediately apparent as a played copy, or moderate / all-around wear. Looks fine sleeved. Fine for players, not for NM/LP collectors.
House line: consistent full-perimeter back edge wear, or significant corner wear (chipped or whitened corners that read as worn, not pin-tips). A crease is not required. Rounded corners are not required.
Also MP: moderate edge wear, light dirt, fading, obvious scratches, a crease, light water marks. Anything that is more than “a few light nicks.”

### Calibration

Nidoran♂, Rising Rivals 72/111 (Doc01560075 / 076). Consistent back-edge wear all around. MP.
Noibat, BREAKthrough 131/162 reverse (Doc01560113 / 114). Significant corners plus full-perimeter back. MP.

### HP. Heavy Play

Heavy wear. Still listable, with an honest HP note.
Significant creasing or folding, heavy whitening, heavy edge wear, severe water damage, and/or tearing, while the card is still intact as a card.

### DMG. Damaged

Below HP. Do not list as a playable copy.
Tears that take a piece out of the card, holes, missing chunks, writing or ink, liquid soak, peeling layers, or anything a buyer would call destroyed.
If it is still a complete card with heavy wear, that is HP. Missing pieces or writing is DMG.

## Cleaning

Do: dry microfiber only. Dust, fingerprints, loose fibres. If specks might be dust, wipe, then reshoot. Do not call dust wear until it survives the wipe.
Do not: liquids, magic eraser, toothpaste, holo buffing, pressing, or any “whitening restoration.” Wear cannot be cleaned. Say so in the listing.

## Holds. Do not list yet

Front and back from different eras (vintage WotC face + modern swirl back). Rescan the matching back. Examples: Natu, Neo Discovery 59/75 (Doc01560011 / 012); Stantler, Neo Genesis 76/111 (Doc01560013 / 014).
Card cannot be identified.
Scan is not a usable listing photo (second card in frame, bed showing). Reshoot; do not treat that sliver as wear.

## Listing notes

One grade only: NM, LP, MP, HP, or DMG.
Name the real flaws. Short, honest, no apology, no hiding.
English cards: English names in the eBay title. Japanese names are catalog-only.
If centering is obviously off, mention it. Do not describe scanner streaks as scratches.

## Calling a grade (checklist)

Identify the card (set, number, holo / reverse / non-holo, language).
Front at card size, then back at card size.
Close look. Do not invent wear from a crop.
Count distinct wear points. NM if under three and closer to NM; LP if three or more light flaws or glance-visible wear; MP if all-around or significant corners.
Check for HP / DMG majors.
Cleaning note. Hold if the pair or ID is wrong.
One-line eBay note.

## Locked calls

Extreme off-centering: listing note only, not a grade hit, unless it looks miscut.
A single light crease on an otherwise clean card: MP. Significant creasing is HP.
Vintage WotC (1999-2003) uses the same wear thresholds as modern cards.
